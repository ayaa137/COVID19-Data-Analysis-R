About the Project

Project Overview

This project investigates how reported COVID-19 cases and deaths evolved during the early pandemic and how later cross-country mortality patterns relate to demographic, health, and socioeconomic characteristics.

Germany is used as the main time-series case study, followed by a comparison with France, Italy, and the United Kingdom. The analysis then expands globally using World Bank development indicators.

Research Question

How did COVID-19 evolve across countries during the early pandemic, and how were pandemic outcomes related to demographic, health, and socioeconomic characteristics?

Data

Two Complementary Datasets

The project combines epidemiological time-series data with country-level socioeconomic indicators to study both epidemic timing and structural country characteristics.

Methodology

Analysis Approach

🧹

Data Preparation

Dates, country names, missing values, reporting corrections, and World Bank indicators were cleaned and standardized before analysis.

📈

Time-Series Analysis

Seven-day moving averages, exponential-growth modeling, turning points, and case-to-death lag optimization were used to study epidemic dynamics.

📊

Cross-Country Analysis

Correlations, PCA, regression, and population-normalized mortality measures were used to compare country characteristics and outcomes.

Insights

Main Findings

📈 Rapid Early Growth

Germany's first wave accelerated rapidly in March 2020, with early reported cases growing approximately 22% per day and doubling in roughly 3.1 days.

⏱️ Cases and Deaths Followed Different Timing

Germany's first-wave data produced an optimized reported case-to-death lag of four days, although this relationship changed substantially in later pandemic periods.

🌍 Country Development Indicators Move Together

GDP, longevity, healthcare capacity, age structure, and urbanization are strongly correlated and form a shared country-development profile.

📊 Socioeconomic Context Explains Only Part of Mortality

The mortality regression achieved an adjusted R² of 0.560, indicating meaningful but incomplete alignment between structural indicators and country-level reported mortality.

Critical Evaluation

Limitations

Reporting Differences

Testing availability, reporting systems, certification practices, and case definitions differed substantially across countries and throughout the pandemic.

Ecological Analysis

Cross-country associations describe country-level patterns and cannot establish individual-level relationships or causal effects.

Missing Indicators

Missing World Bank indicators reduce the number of countries available for complete-case PCA and regression analyses.

Changing Pandemic Conditions

Vaccination, variants, treatment improvements, testing capacity, demographic exposure, and healthcare pressure changed the relationship between cases and deaths over time.
